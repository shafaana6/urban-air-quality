OverviewThis data analytics project examines urban air quality across India and measures its impact on public health outcomes. Using Python, SQL, and Power BI, the project processes data from 2,732 daily readings across 30 major Indian cities (January 2022 – December 2023) to uncover pollution patterns, seasonal trends, and health risks.  Key FindingsNational Baseline: The average national AQI sits at 142.56, which falls into the Unhealthy category.  Peak Pollution: Winter is the most critical season, tracking the highest average AQI (184.3) and driving a spike in hospitalizations (312 daily admissions).  Cleanest Season: Monsoon rains naturally clear the air, dropping the average AQI to 97.4 and reducing hospital admissions to 163 daily.  Highest Risk Zones: Industrial areas are the most polluted, averaging an AQI of 150.3.  Top 3 Most Polluted Cities: Bhopal, Ahmedabad, and Amritsar recorded the highest average AQI levels.  Tools & Technologies UsedPython: Data cleaning, missing value imputation, and Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).  SQL (MySQL/PostgreSQL): Relational database storage and structural business queries.  Power BI: Interactive dashboarding and KPI tracking.  Gamma: AI-driven presentation deck.  Project Pipeline1. Data Cleaning & Engineering (Python)Handled missing data (~4% in AQI, ~3% in public health metrics) using median imputation.  Removed impossible outlier readings ($\text{AQI} > 500$) and corrected negative wind speed values.  Categorized air quality using official CPCB India standard bins.  Engineered a custom health_risk_score metric normalized from 0 to 100.  2. Structured Analysis (SQL)Imported the clean dataset into a database table.  Wrote optimized queries to rank the top 10 most polluted cities, calculate seasonal averages, and compare weekday vs. weekend pollution trends.  3. Interactive Dashboard (Power BI)Built a dynamic dashboard to visually present core insights, featuring:  KPI Cards: Displaying Average AQI (142.56), Total Hospital Admissions (564K), and Hazardous Days (63).  Monthly Trends: Comparing 2022 vs. 2023 pollution curves.  Breakdowns: Donut charts for AQI categories and column charts comparing different zone types (Industrial vs. Coastal).  Core RecommendationsWinter Restrictions: Implement strict industrial and vehicle emissions controls between November and February when AQI peaks.  Healthcare Scaling: Scale up respiratory and cardiovascular medical resources during high-pollution winter months to handle the predictable surge in hospital admissions.
